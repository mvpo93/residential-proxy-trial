## The four different things people mean by "proxy trial"

This distinction matters more than any feature comparison, because the shape of the trial decides what it can actually prove.

- **A no-card trial.** Real residential traffic granted on signup with no payment method. The most buyer-friendly version, and the rarest.
- **A gated trial.** The traffic is free but you go through an identity check, a business email, a community thread, or a conversation with the team. The gate is anti-abuse, and you pay for it in friction rather than money.
- **A permanent free tier.** Not a window at all. Free proxy lists and proxy checkers live here. A free tier cannot sample a paid residential pool, but it does prove your code path, your rotation logic and your parser work before you spend anything.
- **A refund window wearing trial clothes.** "Try risk-free" often means "pay first, claim back within N days under conditions." Read the conditions. A refund policy with usage caps is a purchase with an exit clause.

9Proxy's trial is the **gated** kind. If you want the no-card version, you're looking at a different provider.

## What a trial is actually worth testing

A trial answers exactly one question well: does this pool work on **your** target, **this** week. Spend it on nothing else.

- Fifty to a hundred requests at the real URL you care about, through your real tool, not a speed test.
- The success rate, written down.
- Latency from your own region, since a provider's US performance tells you nothing about your Bangkok connection.
- Country and city targeting verified against an independent IP checker, not against the provider's own lookup page.
- One sticky session pushed through a multi-step login or checkout, if that's what you'll be paying for.
- Gigabytes consumed by those hundred requests. Multiply it. That number is what sizes your first package, and it's the single most useful output of a trial.

What no trial tells you: how the pool behaves at your real volume over several weeks, what support does when something breaks at 2 a.m., and whether the pricing fine print bites later. Those come from the first paid month.

## The other details that decide the purchase

- **IP lifetime.** A residential IP stays alive anywhere from a few hours to about 24 hours, with an average closer to three. If your job needs the same address for a week, residential proxies are the wrong product category, not just the wrong provider.
- **Replacement policy.** If an IP fails within 60 seconds, 9Proxy replaces it automatically. Anything you used in the last 24 hours can be reused free if it comes back online, which matters for daily recurring jobs that hit the same sites.
- **Coverage.** 20M+ residential IPs across 90+ countries. That is not the same as "195 countries," and review coverage has said as much. US, UK, Europe and Southeast Asia are solid; verify niche geographies before committing.
- **Payments.** Credit cards, crypto (USDT, BTC, ETH, LTC, DOGE and others), bank cards, Alipay, Apple Pay and Google Pay.
- **Uptime claim.** 9Proxy advertises 99.95% uptime on cloud-native infrastructure. Treat vendor uptime figures as marketing until your own monitoring agrees.
- **Service history.** Worth knowing before you commit: a user review posted in July 2026 reported 9Proxy down for roughly a week, and third-party trackers reported the service back online in August 2026. Single-user reports aren't proof of anything systemic, but if you're about to move a production workload onto a provider, a recent outage report belongs in your decision.
